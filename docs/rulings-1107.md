## THE READINESS RULINGS

### R1. The plan, said: at intake and on Learn

**What the goal is.** The two chapters are 23 sessions (15 words, 2 asks, 6 places). The six-week tilt says four a week gets there with a week to spare, and the review carries the rest. So, for a trip N days out:

- **The goal = the first min(23, N) sessions of the two chapters, in their order.** Six weeks or more: everything. Fewer days than sessions: as many as one a day reaches, in order, which is why chapter 1's order already puts hello, the numbers, paying, and the counter first.
- **The pace = the goal's sessions spread over the days to the trip, at most one a day.** Four a week at six weeks; six a week at four weeks; one a day under 23 days.
- **The plan starts** at the later of the day the date was set and 42 days before the trip. A learner three months out plays as they like; the BY TODAY line (below) appears when the six weeks begin, and the FOR THE TRIP line is there from the start.

**The intake line**, when the date is set (one line, the numbers filled):

The words, then the sentences: four sessions a week gets you there with time to spare, and the review keeps it fresh.") |

Numbers under ten in words, the rest in figures, as the app already does. No line says what you will not reach; the goal is what you will.

**The goal mark on Learn: two hairlines across the chapter list.** Each is the app's thin gold rule between two rows with a right-aligned micro-caps label:
- **FOR THE TRIP** sits under the goal's last session. When the goal is everything, it sits under chapter 2's last row.
- **BY TODAY** sits under the session the pace says you should have finished by today (the plan's sessions per day times the days since the plan started, rounded down, capped at the goal). Behind, the line sits below your last played row and the gap is visible without a word; ahead, it sits above. No sentence, no colour change, nothing counts the gap. The line is the whole statement.
- Both lines go once the trip date has passed, and BY TODAY goes when it reaches FOR THE TRIP.

### R2. The lapse rule: yes, two rungs, with one guard

Confirmed: **a miss divides the spacing by about five** (85 to 16, 37 to 7, 16 to 3, 7 or less to 1). A slip on a word you own is "check it again," and the ladder says so: an owned word drops from 94 to 76 on the day, not to 38. The strength reads off the new spacing from the day of the miss.

**The guard: two misses in a row go to one day.** One slip is a slip; two is a word you do not hold, and the two-rung rule must not let a word coast down the ladder a rung at a time. Retention's law stands (a miss is a measurement, it stays in).

Stage it now for Tom's weekend; it changes nothing retroactively.

### R4. Readiness as a projection: no, not now

The idea is right in spirit (the dial is named for the trip) and wrong in the number it would show. Read off the brief's ladder: a word at spacing 16 (three right answers, half-life 11 days) projected 35 days out holds 8, under the 0.3 floor; at spacing 37 it holds 35. So for the first three weeks of a steady six-week journey, every word projects at or near the floor and **Readiness reads 15 to 25 for a learner doing everything right**, while the BY TODAY line says they are on pace. The dial and the line would disagree every day until the last fortnight, and the dial would be the one lying by omission (it assumes no further reviews, the only assumption that can be derived, and the one that is false for the steady learner).

What the projection was for is covered elsewhere: "ready versus busy" is the plan line (R1) and Momentum; the honest slide after a drop-off is already the snapshot's behaviour; the cram is now counted once (the due-cap).
