## B. (d) ONE-OFF FORMS: a register, not a debt (amended on Tom's clarification, 11/24)

Chat's first answer made every one-off name a chapter that builds on it. Tom's point is the opposite: a phrase is never taught in order to be built on, and a confident one-off may stay one. What he wants is that a new form is decided on purpose. The rule:

**Before a line brings a new form, in this order:**
1. **Can the learner do the job with a form they already own?** Then use that.
2. **If not, is the phrase worth owning by itself?** Then it is a fixed phrase on a card, never grammar, and it is logged with the reason.
3. **A later chapter may reuse a logged form where its own content calls for it.** No chapter is written to justify one.

A form the learner only hears is logged too: recognition, and one tying sentence at most.

| Form | In chapter 2 | Why it is here | Later use, if any |
|---|---|---|---|
| he + -ado / -ido | said once: Ya he terminado | Nothing owned says "I'm done" (notes 21 and 26). A confident one-off | Chapter 4's "something is wrong", if written. Not required |
| pedir | said once: Perdón, ¿puedo pedir? | One new word inside an owned ask | The same task, if written |
| quiere / quieren | heard twice | The waiter's side of Quiero | The third turn, already planned |
| The staff's le / lo + verb | heard only | The answers to the learner's own asks | None |

**Ya he terminado against the test:** step 1 fails (no owned form says it), step 2 passes. It stays, as a one-off.
