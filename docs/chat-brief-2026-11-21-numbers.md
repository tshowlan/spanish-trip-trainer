# Tripfluent, brief for chat (2026-11-21): numbers in chapter 2. What exists today, and what the two sessions need ruled

*Asked for by the chapter framework (docs/chapter-framework-1121.md): "a numbers brief (what the keypad and the thirteen cover today)". Facts below are read from the live app at v640, not from memory. Five asks at the end.*

## What the learner has today

**The thirteen numbers:** uno to diez, veinte, cincuenta, cien. Nothing else is taught: no teens, no other tens, no compounds.

**Numbers · 1 (chapter 1, "One to ten, and the three bills in your hand."):**
- The set card: all thirteen on one screen, tap a number to hear it. Never a card per number.
- Heard singles on the keypad: a number is spoken, the learner taps its digits. One confusable pair and three others.
- The numeral board: match the spoken or written word to the numeral.
- The reverse keypad: the keys are the words (uno to diez); the learner taps the word for a numeral. This is the only place a number is produced.
- Two bills: a price is spoken, the learner taps the bill (5, 10, 20, 50, 100).
- Three "how many" rungs built on the words' own contexts.

**Numbers · 2 (chapter 1, "A price is said once and then it's gone."):** no new words. Runs of three or four numbers heard in a row at one spot, leaning on the pairs that trip people (seis and siete, dos and diez, cinco and cincuenta, cinco and cien, cincuenta and cien), with rungs and bills between.

## What the keypad can and cannot do

- **Can:** the digits 0 to 9 in a fixed grid with a delete key, so a whole number of any length can be entered; a row of five bills; word keys for one to ten.
- **Cannot today:** anything with two parts. There is no comma or decimal key and no second field, so "dos con cincuenta" (2,50) and "las tres y media" (3:30) have no way in. A clock face or an hour-and-minutes entry does not exist.
- A code comment from 9/20 already expects this: "sesenta and setenta join in chapter 2" as the next confusable pair.

## Where numbers already turn up in chapter 2, untaught or at the edge

**Heard in replies:**
- Son dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, diez, veinte, cincuenta (Counter, Shop, Street, Paying). All inside the thirteen.
- **Son dos con cincuenta** (Counter, Shop): "con cincuenta" is heard and never taught.
- Cinco minutos · Veinte minutos · Todo recto, cinco minutos · Todo recto, diez minutos (Table, Street, Shop, Help).

**Said by the learner:** dos cañas, dos croissants, dos copas de vino, mesa para cuatro, cambio de cincuenta, cambio de cien.

**The hour:** nothing live. ¿A qué hora ___? exists only as an old drill that is off the deck (abre, cierra, sale el tren, empieza). No reply in the live course says "a las ___" or "y media". The Shop's line promises "know when the door is open", and it does that today with abierto and the days, never a time.

## What the framework ruled (so it is not re-argued)

Two ear sessions in the keypad's shape, both chapter 2, both on the plan:
- **Prices you'll hear:** the teens, the tens (veinte to noventa), how they join (treinta y cinco), and "con cincuenta". Seated before Paying sentences, so Paying can stop limiting its prices to the thirteen.
- **The hour:** ¿A qué hora ___? as an ask, with a las ___ and y media. Seated before the Shop.

## Asks

**1. The number set for Prices.** Teens and tens are twenty new words before any compound. Chat lists exactly which are taught as words on the set card, which are only ever heard inside a compound, and the range of prices the session may speak (Code's prompt: whole euros to 99, then "con cincuenta" and "con veinte" as the only cents). Also which new confusable pairs the runs should lean on (sesenta and setenta; dos and doce; tres and trece; the -ce teens against the -nta tens).

**2. How a two-part price is entered.** "Son doce con cincuenta" needs the keypad to take 12,50. Three ways, each a different build: a comma key on the same pad; two taps of meaning (the euros, then pick the cents from con veinte / con cincuenta / nothing); or multiple choice among three written prices. Chat picks the shape; Code mocks it for Tom before building (a look change).

**3. The hour's shape.** What the learner does with a heard time: tap it on the keypad as digits (9, then 30?), pick a clock, or pick among written times. And the set: the hours one to twelve with "a las" and "a la una", "y media", and whether "y cuarto", "menos cuarto", mediodía, and the 24-hour form Spain prints on doors (Abre a las 17:00) are in.

**4. ¿A qué hora ___? as an ask.** Its card, its fills (abre, cierra, and what else passes the chapter 2 test), and where its sentences sit: in The hour session itself, or in the Shop after it. To know: the Shop is ten sentences today and Paying thirteen, both under the sixteen ceiling.

**5. What changes elsewhere once these exist.** Paying and the other places may then speak prices beyond the thirteen: chat says whether existing reply sets are rewritten with real prices (Son doce con cincuenta) or left, and whether Numbers · 2's line ("A price is said once and then it's gone.") moves to the new session.
