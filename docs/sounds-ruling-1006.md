# Tripfluent — Sounds you'll say: the pronunciation check and the say-line law (2026-10-06)

*Answers the 10/06 handoff. Part 1 rules HOW pronunciations are written (the thing to settle before any row). Part 2 checks the nine rows. Part 3 is the voice pass.*

## PART 1 — THE SAY-LINE LAW (how we write pronunciation, app-wide)

**What a learner sees:** the say line only. IPA lives in the pack as the backing and never renders; the es-ES voice is the truth the learner hears, the say line is the eye's helper.

**The convention, fixed:**
1. **Parts divided by hyphens, the stressed part in CAPITALS.** One part per syllable. (Code's rule, confirmed.)
2. **One spelling per Spanish sound, always.** English respelling is ambiguous ("a" is cat or father), so the vowels are fixed: **a = ah · e = eh · i = ee · o = oh · u = oo.** Never "ay", "ee-ah", or any English rhyme that imports a glide. This is the one correction to Code's draft: the lines mix "GRA" and "ka" (bare a) with "BEE" and "OH" (keyed). Every line uses the key.
3. **The consonant key:** θ = **th** · x = **h** (the rule line says it is rougher) · ɲ = **ny** · ʝ = **y** · tap ɾ = **r** · trill r = **rr** · k always **k** (never c or qu) · b/v = **b**.
4. **No silent letters in a say line.** hola is OH-lah, never HO-lah with a note.
5. **Glides are written as they are said:** cia = thyah, quie = kyeh, ue = weh. A say line never splits a glide into two parts.
6. **The say line is for the eye at a glance** and is always an approximation; the card says so once, nowhere else: "Said the Spain way. The voice is the real thing."

**Where it renders:** the key-sound cards (this session), and later any phrase card that carries a say line. One convention, no per-surface variants.

## PART 2b — ISOLATING THE KEY SOUND (the gold, the confirm, the wall)

Tom's catch: "What is the key sound in gracias?" with **thee / see** tests a syllable gracias does not contain (gra-cias says thyahs, never thee). The ear learns "thee", the word says something else. The rule that fixes it:

**THE GOLD IS THE LETTER; THE TILE IS ITS SOUND.** Gold marks only the letters that make the key sound (the **c** in gracias, not cia; the **j** in jamón; the **h** in hola; the **ll** in calle; the **ñ** in baño; the **v** in vino; the **qu** in quiero; the **rr** in perro). A tile renders that sound alone, in the key, and nothing around it: **th / s · h / j · y / l · ny / n · b / v · k / kw · rr / r.** A tile never renders a syllable unless the syllable IS the sound.

Two exceptions, both because the sound is not a letter:
- **hola (silence):** a tile cannot say "silent", so the pair is the word's first part with and without the h: **oh / hoh**.
- **café (stress):** stress belongs to the whole word, so the pair is two whole say lines: **kah-FEH / KAF-fay**.

**The two surfaces, now distinct:**
- **The card's confirm** ("Tap the pronunciation") shows two WHOLE say lines that differ only in the gold part: GRAH-**thyahs** / GRAH-**see-ahs**, the gold carried into both. The learner hears the word whole and picks the whole.
- **The wall** ("Find the sound") shows the SOUND alone: th / s. The learner isolates.
Card = the word; wall = the sound. Neither shows a syllable fragment like thee.
