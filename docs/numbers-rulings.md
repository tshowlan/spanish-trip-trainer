## 1. THE KEYPAD

**Name:** the keypad (plain). **One surface, three stimuli, and the digits never move**: dialer order, 1-2-3 / 4-5-6 / 7-8-9 / 0 centered on the bottom row, the two bottom corners empty (they become "Show it again" / "Continue" homes, never digits). Keys 64px minimum, 10px gaps, the shipped tile chrome (raised, push-down press). Under the anchored layout: the stimulus sits at 16% (the audio control for heard forms; the run's single spot for flashed forms), the keypad fills the answers zone. The thumb learns positions; the difficulty is Spanish.

**Direction lines (the ask grammar's labels):**
- Heard single: **You hear · Tap the number**
- The run: **You hear · Tap all three, in order** (flashed: **You read · Tap all three, in order**)
- The reverse keypad: **Say · seven** (the keys are the words; with sound on, a tapped key speaks)
- Heard price (the bills): **You hear · Tap the bill** — the keypad becomes a row of five: 5 · 10 · 20 · 50 · 100, drawn as bill faces (tokens only, no currency art); the only variant where keys are not digits.

Wrong taps wear wrong-then-release (900ms → dim); a miss never re-serves in the session (misses inform); the correct key washes green on resolve.

## 2. THE SET CARD

One screen, present-card grammar. Label **NUMBERS** · title **"One to ten, and the bills."** · a 2×5 grid of chips, each **numeral + word** (1 uno … 10 diez), tap any to hear it · below, a second row labeled **IN YOUR HAND**: **20 veinte · 50 cincuenta · 100 cien**, tap to hear. One line under the grid, voice-lawful: **"You will hear these far more than you say them."** No card per number, ever.

## 3. THE RUN

- **One spot** at 16%: each word replaces the last in the same place, ~450ms per word [tune], a short fade between (light turns on, never travels). Not three at once — succession is the point.
- **"Show it again"** sits where "Tap to hear it again" sits (the hint line under the stimulus); tapping it replays the run and records a supported rep, never a fail. No countdown, no timer visible.
- **Resolve:** after the third tap, the three words appear together in the played-line grammar with their digits — **siete · tres · ocho → 7 · 3 · 8** — then Continue. On a miss, the same reveal shows which position went wrong (the digit-level diff: the missed position dims, the rest hold).
- **Selection:** neighbor pairs, never random — seis/siete · dos/diez · cinco/cincuenta · uno/once · later sesenta/setenta. **Adaptive:** the pace tightens and the run grows to four as runs clear; it eases on a miss. Silent mode skips the singles and runs the flashed twin.

## 4. THE SOUND-OFF DOOR LINE

Yes, one line, literal, no nag: **"Numbers are an ear skill. This one is better with sound on."** It rides the door's status line when the session opens silent, and nowhere else.
