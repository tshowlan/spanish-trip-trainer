# Tripfluent — The Beginning of the Journey: Rulings (2026-09-11)

*One surface open: the first ten sessions. Built on Tom's rulings (rooms of two machines; the cuts Asking for things / Finding out; ~4.5 minutes, short and punchy; kits split; depth to the returns). Code's fact base is the ground: First words = 55 beats / ten minutes, batched by type; machines 11-13 beats on one frame, seven in a row.*

## THE DIAGNOSIS, NAMED

The composer batches kit lessons by exercise type (13 presents → 11 MCs → 9 fills → 13 colds). The lesson-breath canvas already rules the opposite: each item climbs its own ladder, new among returning. Enforcing that law is half of this session's fix; the other half is the rooms.

## RULING 1 — THE ROOMS (labels as copy)

Two rooms, Tom's cuts, plain English (the naming law):
- **Asking for things** — Wantmachine (Quiero), Bringmachine (Me puede traer), Needmachine (Necesito).
- **Finding out** — Findmachine (¿Dónde está?), Theremachine (¿Hay?), Pricemachine (¿Cuánto cuesta?), Whenmachine (¿A qué hora?).

Door grammar: the room is the name (logo faces: "Asking" + *for things* / "Finding" + *out*); the label line carries the pair (MACHINES: WANT · BRING). Lessons per room:
- **Asking for things · 1** = Want + Bring · **Asking for things · 2** = Need, SOLO at the full single-machine arc (the safety machine — médico, farmacia — earns undivided attention; it is not smuggled into a return).
- **Finding out · 1** = Find + There · **Finding out · 2** = Price + When.
Four machine lessons cover all seven; no machine arrives inside a return.

## RULING 2 — THE SHORT ARC (a pair of machines in ~4.5 minutes)

Per machine, in order: **forge-present (1) · conveyor ×3 cues (3) · weld ×2 (2) · exchange ×1 (1)** = 7 beats; the second machine begins after the first's exchange, its own forge marking the frame change. Then **the room's close: one lap across both frames** (one rep each, in the chapter's highest allowed form — scaffolded in chapter one, per Ruling 6). ~15 beats, ~4.5 minutes at the measured medians.

**What moves to the returns (the depth):** lap 2 (shuffled cues), the stretch ×2, the remaining exchanges (2-3 per machine from the banked 28), and the remaining welds ×2 — the return door's "Run the lap" and the machine shop's circuit serve them. The 17-beat machine lesson (depth ruling 2) is superseded for rooms; the solo Need lesson keeps the full arc.

## RULING 3 — KITS INTERLEAVE PER ITEM (the canvas enforced)

**AMENDED BY TOM AT PLAY (2026-09-12, staging):** the intro is not per item. A kit half introduces every phrase first (cards back to back), then the PAIRS board is the first exercise, then one scaffolded rung per phrase, the lap, the close. Guessing an exact word straight off a card is out.

A kit half runs item by item: **present · grasp · one scaffolded rung (fill or build)** for phrase 1, then phrase 2, and so on — new phrases arriving among ones just met (the weave). **The lap runs every phrase once, fast, in the lesson's highest allowed form** — no cold rung inside the item's climb, and (Ruling 6) no cold typing at all in chapter one: the lap is tiles or fills there. 6-7 phrases × 3 beats + the lap = 24-28 beats, ~5 minutes. Thirteen of anything in a row never happens again.

## RULING 4 — THE KIT SPLITS

Any kit over 8 phrases splits into halves of 6-7 by natural subgroup; Code proposes the split lines from the item lists, chat names them. Titles: **"First words · 1" / "First words · 2"** (the label grammar, micro-caps friendly; no "part two" prose). Numbers that pay, When it goes wrong, and Where's the loo split the same way if over 8.

## RULING 5 — "YOUR FIRST MACHINE" RIDES THE FIRST ROOM

The one-line intro ("Your first machine: one frame, many asks.") appears on the first forge-present of Asking for things · 1 and nowhere else. The second machine in the room gets no intro; the name-it-once law holds. Each machine's name appears at its own first meeting (the forge-present label: BRINGMACHINE), per the friction map's row.

## RULING 6 — COLD PRODUCTION WAITS FOR CHAPTER TWO (Tom)

Cold typing is tedious before a learner has a base, so the climb is paced across chapters, not inside week one. **Chapter one: no cold typing anywhere** — lessons, laps, closes, or return-door laps run in scaffolded forms (tiles, fills). **Chapter two: cold enters in the lap only** (the close's fast round). **Chapter three onward: the full ladder** [tune]. The constitution's "production is the proof; cold is the summit" holds unchanged — the summit is reached across the six weeks, and the tilt still ends in rehearsal. The input climb's expertise thresholds sit under this chapter floor, never above it.

## RULING 7 — DIFFICULTY IS FLAT WITHIN A LESSON (Tom)

A lesson feels incongruent if it gets harder as it goes. So: **a lesson's production form is fixed at entry** — the chapter floor (Ruling 6) and the learner's expertise decide it once — and it holds through every beat and the close. Tiles all the way, or keyboard all the way; never tiles that turn into a keyboard mid-lesson. The ladder's rungs (meet, grasp, place) are ROLES at one level, not levels. **The input climb steps between lessons, never within one.** Any harder form is a named choice at the door ("Cold lap" as an explicit option, the way Duolingo names hard mode), never a mid-lesson surprise.
