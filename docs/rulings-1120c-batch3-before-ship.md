The four-or-more threshold was tied to nothing but chat's guess, and it broke on the first learner it met. Replace it with a rule that does not depend on the session's size:

**A learner with two or more Table lines of their own gets them as "Your table lines." A single line stays woven.**

- Vegetarian (three lines and the ¿Lleva carne? card): own row. Their Table is fifteen, like everyone's.

## 2. "SÍ, AHORA": out. Tom's ear is right, and vale takes its place

Chat wrote "Sí, ahora / Yes, now" on 11/01 and cannot source the clipped form as waiter speech. The English is not something anyone says. What is documented, and what Tom heard all week, is vale: the everyday "okay" of Spain, including from a waiter taking an order.

| Line | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Hay pan para la mesa? (live) | Sí, ahora | **Vale** / Okay |
| ¿Me puede traer la carta, por favor? (live) | Sí, ahora | **Vale, un segundo** / Okay, one second |
| Queremos agua sin gas para la mesa, por favor. (staged; was the Quiero line, live) | Sí, ahora | **Vale** / Okay |
| Llame a la policía, por favor. (live) | Vale, ahora | **Vale, ya llamo** / Okay, I'm calling now |

- The other two replies in each set stand. The water line's set is ¿Una o dos? · Vale · Marchando; chat's unused drafts (¿Grande o pequeña?, ¿Fría o del tiempo?) stay unused.
- **Vale does not join the stock list.** Stock is an exact string heard on six or more sentences, and the ceiling exists to stop one reply answering everything. Vale is spread across forms instead (Vale · Vale, un segundo · Vale, perfecto · Vale, lo digo en cocina), which is how it is heard: as the first word of many answers.
- **Ahora mismo is not cut.** It is real Spain speech and Tom's memory is a weight, not a transcript. The weight applies going forward: in the next Table batches, where a waiter simply acknowledges, the reply is a vale form before it is another Ahora mismo.

## 3. "¿DE LA CASA?": out. The follow-up to a glass of red is ¿Rioja o Ribera?

Code's instinct is right and it is well sourced: Spanish wine writers call ¿Rioja o Ribera? the classic question you hear on ordering a glass of wine in most of the country's bars. "Vino de la casa" is a real phrase on menus and in a diner's own order; chat had no source for it as the waiter's question.

On "Queremos dos copas de vino.":

```js
replies: [["¿Tinto o blanco?", "Red or white?"], ["¿Rioja o Ribera?", "Rioja or Ribera?"], ["Muy bien", "Very good"]], note: "Rioja and Ribera are the two red wine regions nearly every bar pours. Asked ¿Rioja o Ribera?, either answer gets you a glass of red."
```

- Heard only: Rioja, Ribera (names; the English carries them). "de la casa" leaves the heard-only list.
- Spain pack only. One caveat for Tom's ear: this is documented for Spain at large, and Barcelona bars may as easily offer a Catalan red by name. If that is what he heard, the reply changes to match.
