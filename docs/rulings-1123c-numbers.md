## A. THE SHAPE, AND TWO AMENDMENTS

**Three sessions, not two.** Prices you'll hear is one row in the framework and two sittings in the app, by the app's own "· Part 1 / · Part 2" convention: eleven new number words, the join, and the cents do not fit one sitting. The hour is the third. The plan's goal grows by three.

- **Prices you'll hear · Part 1:** the tens, how they join, and the cents.
- **Prices you'll hear · Part 2:** eleven to fifteen, the rest of the teens and the twenties by ear, and prices that mix everything.
- **The hour:** a las ___, a la una, y media. No new number words.

**Amendment 1 (to 9/20 §7, "ordered taps").** A two-part number is entered with a separator key, not two unmarked taps. Digit-by-digit entry (Tom, 9/22) made the old ruling unbuildable: the pad cannot know where the euros end.

**Amendment 2 (to 9/20 §5, Numbermachine).** These are ear sessions on the keypad. The "___ y ___" build conveyor is not part of them and stays parked. Two 9/20 rules carry over unchanged: once to quince are five words with one line ("Eleven to fifteen have their own words."), and fused forms are met by ear in runs, never taught as a rule.

**Seating, as three constraints** (chat has the Learn order only as of 10/28: Street, Shop, Help, Paying; Code seats to the live order):
1. Both Prices parts sit before the first session that speaks a price beyond the thirteen. Paying is last, so "before Paying" holds either way.
2. Prices · Part 2 sits before The hour (once and doce are met there).
3. The hour sits directly before Shop sentences.
Chat's seating if the order is still 10/28's: Part 1 before Street sentences; Part 2 and The hour before Shop sentences. This moves Prices earlier than the framework's "before Paying", because the Shop asks prices too.

## B. ASK 1: THE NUMBER SET

**Part 1, the set card.** Title **"The tens, and how a price is built."** One row of eight chips, numeral + word, tap to hear: 20 veinte · 30 treinta · 40 cuarenta · 50 cincuenta · 60 sesenta · 70 setenta · 80 ochenta · 90 noventa (veinte and cincuenta are owned; six new). Below, two worked examples as chips: **35 treinta y cinco** · **3,50 tres con cincuenta**. One line: **"Y joins a number. Con starts the cents."**

**Part 1 may speak:** whole tens; joined numbers 31 to 99; prices with con, the cents always a whole ten (con diez to con noventa), con cincuenta in about half.
**Part 1 leans on:** sesenta / setenta · cuarenta / cincuenta · tres / treinta · cuatro / cuarenta · cinco / cincuenta · ocho / ochenta · nueve / noventa · and **y against con** (treinta y cinco is 35; treinta con cincuenta is 30,50).

**Part 2, the set card.** Title **"Eleven to fifteen have their own words."** Five chips: 11 once · 12 doce · 13 trece · 14 catorce · 15 quince. No second line.

**Heard only, never on a card (Part 2 runs):** dieciséis to diecinueve; veintidós to veintinueve; ciento before a number (101 to 199, two runs at most).
**Part 2 may speak:** everything in Part 1, plus 11 to 29, plus one cents form ending in five: **con noventa y cinco** (what a menu prints as ,95; chat's own observation, unsourced). In its last runs, the same price with con dropped (doce cincuenta).
**Part 2 leans on:** dos / doce · tres / trece · trece / treinta · catorce / cuarenta · quince / cincuenta · cinco / quince · once / doce · dieciséis / diecisiete · dieciséis / sesenta · diecisiete / setenta · dos con cincuenta / doce con cincuenta.

**Never spoken in either part:** a price ending in one euro (1, 21, 31 ...). Uno changes before euros (un euro, treinta y un euros) and that is not this session's job.
**"euros"** may be heard after a whole price (Son veinte euros), never after con, never needed for the entry. It stays glue.

**Two Good to know lines, each once, on the reveal of the first run that shows it:**
- Part 2, first dropped con: **"Con is often dropped. Doce cincuenta is still 12,50."**
- Part 2, first ciento: **"Cien alone is 100. With a number after it, it is ciento: ciento veinte."**

**Sourcing:** con for the cents is the standard form in ELE material from Spain ("treinta y nueve euros con noventa", ProfeDeELE; "tres euros con cuarenta", Transparent). The dropped con is sourced in a Spain market dialogue ("Uno cincuenta el kilo", Open University). Everything else is ordinary numeral usage.

## C. ASK 2: THE TWO-PART PRICE IS A COMMA KEY

**One key, in the pad's empty bottom-left corner: a comma.** "Doce con cincuenta" is tapped 1 · 2 · , · 5 · 0.

- One mechanic for every number in the app. No second field, no multiple choice.
- The comma is what Spain prints (12,50 €), so the entry looks like the receipt.
- Tapping the comma is the learner saying "that was con." It is the thing being taught.
- **Rules:** a whole price takes no comma. After a comma, exactly two digits. The readout shows the comma and a trailing €.
- **Direction line (9/20's):** You hear · Tap the price.
- Multiple choice was the cheapest build and is declined: one guess in three is not ear training.

Code mocks the key for Tom (a look change). If the corner is not free on some rung, Code says so in the mock.

## D. ASK 3: THE HOUR

**The same pad, the same corner: the key reads ":" in this session.** "A las ocho y media" is tapped 8 · : · 3 · 0.

- **Rule:** always hour, colon, two digits. "A las nueve" is 9:00. One rule, no guessing when the entry is done.
- **Direction line:** You hear · Tap the time.
- **The set card.** Title **"The hour, as it is said."** Chips: a la una · a las dos · a las tres ... a las doce, and one more: **y media**. One line: **"Doors print 17:00. People say a las cinco."** (True of Spain; chat's own knowledge, not searched. Tom was there last week and is the check.)
- **The board (the numeral board's mechanic, six pairs):** the said hour against the printed one. a la una ↔ 13:00 · a las dos ↔ 14:00 · a las cinco ↔ 17:00 · a las ocho y media ↔ 20:30 · a las nueve y media ↔ 9:30 · a las diez ↔ 10:00.
- **In the set:** the twelve hours, a la una against a las, y media.
- **Out of chapter 2:** y cuarto, menos cuarto, mediodía, de la mañana / de la tarde. Opening hours fall on the hour or the half. They go to chapter 4's "a table for tonight" task, where a booking can land on a quarter.
- **Leans on:** a la una / a las once · a las dos / a las doce · a las seis / a las siete · the hour against the hour y media.

## E. ASK 4: ¿A QUÉ HORA ___?

**The card and its two lines sit in Shop sentences, directly after The hour.** The hour stays a pure ear session; the ask lives where the door is. A place-frame card, like ¿Me pone? at the Counter. Composer tag `a que hora`. The Shop goes from ten sentences to twelve.

```js
{ card: true, es: "¿A qué hora ___?", en: "What time ___?", words: [["A qué hora", "what time"]], contextEs: "¿A qué hora cierra?", contextEn: "What time do you close?", note: "The answer comes back the same way: a las ocho, at eight. One o'clock is a la una." },
{ frame: "a que hora", es: "¿A qué hora cierra?", en: "What time do you close?", hint: [["What time", "a qué hora"], ["do you close?", "cierra"]], near: ["What time do you open?", "Are you open today?"], replies: [["A las ocho y media", "At eight thirty"], ["A las dos, y abrimos a las cinco", "At two, and we open at five"], ["A las nueve", "At nine"]], note: "Many small shops close at two and open again at five. Ask before you walk back." },
{ frame: "a que hora", es: "¿A qué hora abre la farmacia?", en: "What time does the pharmacy open?", hint: [["What time", "a qué hora"], ["does it open", "abre"], ["the pharmacy?", "la farmacia"]], near: ["What time does the pharmacy close?", "Where is the pharmacy?"], replies: [["A las nueve y media", "At nine thirty"], ["A las cinco", "At five"], ["Ya está abierta", "It's already open"]] }
```

- **Card pair:** What time ___? / How much is ___?
- **Gloss:** a qué hora is one piece, "what time" (10/19).
- **Fills that pass the chapter 2 test:** cierra and abre, and nothing else. Out: sale el tren and empieza (the phone and the ticket show them; a question is taught only where the answer is not in front of the learner); breakfast and check-out (the hotel desk gets nothing).
- **Two situations each for the which-one bank, if a place frame joins it** (the want stated, never "what time"): In a shop · "It's late and you're still looking. You don't want to be locked in." | At the pharmacy door · "It's shut. You'll come back when it isn't." | At the bakery · "You want bread tomorrow, before your train." | At the market · "The stalls are packing up. You'll try again tomorrow."
- **Sourcing:** the frame and a las ___ are textbook-standard. The midday-closing note and all six replies are chat's own, unsourced, for Tom's ear. abrimos and abierta are heard only.

**Out, on Tom's word (11/23):** "¿A qué hora abre la cocina?" (the kitchen that opens at 20:30 while the bar is already serving). Tom did not meet a closed kitchen in Barcelona, so it fails the chapter 2 test and is not written.
