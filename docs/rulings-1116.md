## P1. THE STREET ASSUMES A PHONE IN THE HAND: yes

**Premise, for the constitution:** *The traveler has a map in their hand. The app never teaches asking a stranger where something is or how far. It teaches the two things a phone cannot do: confirm with a person, and tell a driver.*

What that rules:
- **The three cuts stand:** ¿Está lejos la estación?, ¿Está cerca el hotel?, ¿Hay taxis en la estación? Nobody asked them, and the last one has no one to ask.
- **What ¿Está ___? is for now: the state of a thing in front of you.** Open, closed, free, taken, included (¿Está abierto?, ¿Está libre?, ¿Está la propina en la cuenta?). Far and near leave the frame as questions about places; lejos and cerca stay as heard words, because the answer to "is it this way?" still comes back with them. The está/hay adverb notes of 11/05 retire with the sentences they were written for; the word-order re-show goes with them.
- **The Street's lines become confirming and pointing.** Shapes for batch 2 (lines written in its handoff): the confirm ("Is it this way?", phone in hand), and the point ("Here, please." to a driver, "To this address, please.").
- **The taxi card of 11/5b and 11/6 is retired.** It named a destination; the job is showing one. The a-line survives as the driver's line with the phone held up, and with a name in the slot ("To the Hotel ___, please."), which is what Tom actually said. The Street's taxi exception to the one-frame rule stands unchanged; the pick-up confirmation ("¿Tom?" "Sí, soy yo.") stays parked for transport in chapter 3.
- Also retired with the premise, to check in batch 2: any remaining Street sentence whose only listener is a stranger being asked for a location. ¿Dónde está el baño? stays everywhere (indoors, no map knows).

## P2. ORDERING FOR THE TABLE: the Table's frame grows a slot; no second frame

The Table's frame was never really "Para mí"; it is **Para ___,** and Tom's week found its other fills:

One frame, three fills, the one-frame law intact, and the menu word and the ordering line turn out to be the same two words.

**"We will have"** is not a frame either: it is the plural of an ask the learner owns. **Queremos ___** (we want) sits under Quiero with a card of its own word, the way pago does below. **"Can I order from you?"** sits under ¿Puedo? (the lines in batch 3's handoff). The rule this sets, for the constitution: *an ask may be said for the table (queremos, nos); that is the same ask, plural, taught once with a card, never a new frame.*

## P3. CATALAN: a layer over Spain, read and heard, never said. Yes.

- **The principle:** a layer, never a fork. The Spain pack stays one pack; a learner without the answer gets none of it. This is the register rule applied to a second language: *the mouth keeps one language; the ear and the eye are told the other.* Castilian is answered everywhere in Barcelona.
- **The intake question: "Where in Spain?"** with exactly the answers that change something: **Barcelona and Catalonia** · **Somewhere else** · skip. Madrid and Andalusia are not options until an answer moves content (the intake law: a question moves a weight, sets a parameter, or is cut). Valencia, the Basque Country, and Galicia join as answers the day their layers exist. It is a trip question, asked at trip creation beside the date.
- **What the layer holds: signs and the heard greetings, both.** Tom read the first and heard the second; a layer with only signs would have left "bon dia" unexplained every morning.
  - Signs: a second face on the plates that have one (the Catalan under the Castilian, as the doors show them), not new items. Lavabos and Serveis join the bathroom door; Tom's note 6 says Lavabos is the commoner one, so it leads.
  - Heard: a handful of greetings as heard-only words in Words you'll hear (bon dia first). The learner answers in Castilian, and the card says so.
  - One Good to know, once, on the first Catalan the learner meets: **"Barcelona's signs come in two languages, and the Catalan is often the only one. You speak Castilian. Everyone answers it."**
- **Where it lives: inside the existing sessions, not a session of its own.** A sign is learned at the door it hangs on. A learner who sets Barcelona after playing Signs gets the new faces as cards gained since (already built).
- The word list (which plates, which greetings, spellings) is batch 8's handoff and gets verified there against Barcelona signage, not written from memory here.

## BATCH 1, WRITTEN: Paying

**Verified:** ¿Quiere copia? (or ¿Quieres copia?) is the standard question after a card payment in Spain; the copy is the card terminal's slip. Sources: [Banco de España, "Pago con tarjeta: ¿Quieres copia?"](https://clientebancario.bde.es/pcb/es/blog/pago-con-tarjeta--quieres-copia-.html?blog=%2Fwebcb%2FBlog) · [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/no-no-necesito-copia-que-pleno-2021-redundante-pedir-comprobante-fisico-cuando-pagamos-tarjeta-1/amp).

### 1a. Where you pay: one card, two sentences

pago is a new word, so it gets a card (new words on cards only). The frame tag `pago` is for the composer; it is a fixed line like Está bien así, not a taught frame.

```js
          { card: true, es: "¿Pago aquí?", en: "Do I pay here?", words: [["Pago", "do I pay"], ["aquí", "here"]], contextEs: "¿Pago aquí o en la barra?", contextEn: "Do I pay here or at the bar?", note: "Pagar is to pay, and pago is I pay. Two words, and the question is in your voice." },
          { frame: "pago", es: "¿Pago aquí?", en: "Do I pay here?", hint: [["Do I pay", "pago"], ["here?", "aquí"]], near: ["Can I pay here?", "Do I pay at the bar?"], replies: [["Sí, aquí", "Yes, here"], ["No, en la barra", "No, at the bar"], ["En caja", "At the register"]] },
          { frame: "pago", es: "¿Pago aquí o en la barra?", en: "Do I pay here or at the bar?", hint: [["Do I pay", "pago"], ["here", "aquí"], ["or", "o"], ["at the bar?", "en la barra"]], near: ["Do I pay here or at the register?", "Can I pay by card at the bar?"], replies: [["Aquí mismo", "Right here"], ["En la barra", "At the bar"], ["Donde quiera", "Wherever you like"]] },
```

- Seat: after the ¿Me cobra? card and its builds, before the card and cash sentences (find where, then pay).
- New said words: pago, la barra. Heard only: caja, mismo, donde quiera. Three meanings per set, none stock.
- "At the front" is en caja to a Spaniard (the register by the door); it is taught as the answer you hear, which is how Tom met it.

### 1b. The copy: heard, on the card sentence

The learner never says it; they hear it with the terminal in the waiter's hand and answer with two words they own. So it rides the card sentence as a reply and a note, not a new item:

```js
          { frame: "puedo", es: "¿Puedo pagar la cuenta con tarjeta?", en: "Can I pay the check by card?", hint: [["Can I", "puedo"], ["pay", "pagar"], ["the check", "la cuenta"], ["by card?", "con tarjeta"]], near: ["Can I pay the check in cash?", "Could you bring me the check?"], replies: [["Sí, claro", "Yes, of course"], ["Solo efectivo", "Cash only"], ["Sí. ¿Quiere copia?", "Yes. Do you want a copy?"]], note: "After the card goes through you will hear ¿Quiere copia?: do you want the receipt copy. No, gracias is the usual answer; Sí, por favor gets you the slip." },
```

- This replaces the sentence's current third reply. Heard-only word: copia. The reply is the sentence's own (it only follows a card).
- The floor's picker should treat "Sí. ¿Quiere copia?" as owed on the first play of Paying, since it is the reason the batch exists; Code's call on how.
- Paying's count: 14 sentences, three cards (¿Me cobra?, ¿Pago aquí?, Está bien así). The session's line becomes: **"How to find where you pay, ask for the check, and handle the change."**
