# Signs as signs (Tom + Code, 2026-09-26; STAGED "sign-plates", v369)

**Why:** a sign has nothing to say in a sentence ("Cerrado. Vale." was thin). A sign is something you see, then act on. So the read sessions show the word as a PLATE and the exercise is the situation.

**The plate:** one component, two shades by theme. Dark theme: the door plate (dark, cream letters; "too much white for someone choosing a dark screen", Tom). Light theme: enamel, a deeper cream than the page with a navy edge, so it lifts off the cream ground.

**Signs you'll read:** the card = plate + meaning + where you'd see it (the two-word contexts retire from the card). Rungs rotate per sign: read the sign then act (a situation, two actions) · which sign do you follow (a need, three plates) · read the sign, pick its meaning. Lap: the board turned around.

**Days you'll read:** seven day cards as plates, the words board, then the timetables: real Horario plates in the 24-hour clock (a shop with the afternoon close; a museum shut Mondays; a bakery open Sunday mornings), one situation each ("Tuesday, 15:30. You:"), the SAME three answers every time (Go in · Come back later · Closed today) so no answer quotes the plate; plates vary so a day's calendar slot is no shortcut. Each situation records against its day. Lap: the board turned around.

**Copy (Code's drafts for chat's pass):** the places, the situations and actions, the needs, the three fixed answers, the timetables. In curriculum.js (w-signs items: place / act / need; w-days: tables).
