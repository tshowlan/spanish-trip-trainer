## 6. THE RULE (Tom's test, for the constitution)

**A Good to know on a dietary line does one of three things: it teaches a Spanish word, it says something true of Spain in particular, or it vouches that this is the phrase said here. It never explains the learner's condition to them. A note that cannot be sourced is cut, not softened.**

## 2. THE FRYER SENTENCE: Tom's way wins. ¿La freidora es compartida?

Chat picked aparte for a teaching reason, not a usage one, and Tom was right to ask. Checked against Spanish celiac writing:

- **compartida is the common word.** Spanish celiac guides tell diners to ask whether the fryer is shared, and name "freidora compartida" as the thing to ask about in a restaurant.
- **exclusiva is the word for the good answer** (a fryer used for nothing else): the Madrid association's kitchen requirements, restaurant write-ups, and a long-running celiac forum all use it.
- **aparte is real but rare** (one diner review). It stays as a word you hear.

So the learner asks the question the way it is asked, and hears the two words for the answer they want:

```js
          { frame: "freidora", es: "¿La freidora es compartida?", en: "Is the fryer shared?", hint: [["Is", "es"], ["the fryer", "la freidora"], ["shared?", "compartida"]], near: ["Is the fryer separate?", "Is the kitchen shared?"], replies: [["Sí, es la misma", "Yes, it's the same one"], ["No, tenemos una exclusiva", "No, we have a dedicated one"], ["Lo pregunto en cocina", "I'll ask the kitchen"]], note: "Compartida is shared. The answer you want is No, with exclusiva, a fryer used for nothing else, or aparte, separate." },
```

- The frame tag is for the composer; it is a fixed question. It replaces ¿Tiene freidora aparte? for celiac, fish, and shellfish.
- **Yes is now the answer you do not want**, which matches the other two questions in the set (¿Lleva gluten?, ¿Hay contaminación cruzada?). All three read the same way: Sí is the warning.
- Fish and shellfish keep the line: allergy guidance in Spanish says to be wary of fried food because restaurants cook different foods, shellfish included, in the same oil.

- **The milk note:** Code's draft claimed sin lactosa gets offered for a milk allergy in Spain. Chat could not source that, so it is not in the note. What is left passes the test as a word note: two Spanish labels an English eye reads as the same thing.
- **The egg note:** sourced both ways. Traditional allioli has no egg and purists say so loudly; everyday recipes include one, and a Catalan health service's egg-allergy leaflet lists alioli beside mayonnaise among sauces to check. "Often," not "usually."

## THE TEN THAT PASS: three small fixes

Agreed on all ten verdicts. Reading them in the list:

1. **A grammar bug in the ¿Lleva ___? card note** when the slot is plural: "asks whether nuts is in it" / "peanuts is in it." Slot-proof ending: **"Llevar means to carry, as in para llevar. A dish carries its ingredients, so ¿Lleva frutos secos? asks if the dish has nuts."** (template: "asks if the dish has ___.")
2. **The beer note** overstates: **"Caña is a draft beer. Gluten-free beer usually comes in a bottle, so ask for cerveza."**
3. **The vegetarian note** rests on a usage claim chat cannot source ("carne often means red meat here"). Keep the part that is certain and still does the job: **"Jamón is ham and atún is tuna. Neither is always counted as carne, so ask for them by name."**
