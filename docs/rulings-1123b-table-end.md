## THE SHAPE, FIRST

The end of a meal in Spain is mostly heard, and the learner already owns the answers (Sí, gracias · No, gracias · the check line). What the pack lacks is recognition of three things the staff say: postre, terminado, retirar. So the batch is small on purpose: **one word, one sentence, two dietary lines.** Nothing gracious; that is chapter 3.

## 1. THE DESSERT QUESTION: a taught word, then an owed reply. The batch does not wait for the third turn.

- **postre is a taught word** (ask 6), and its context line is the waiter's question. The word is the constant; the phrasing around it varies by waiter (¿Postre o café? · ¿Algo de postre? · ¿Van a tomar postre?), so the learner is taught the word and hears one full form.
- **The question is then heard as an owed reply** on the new sentence in §3, the way ¿Quiere copia? is met in Paying: heard once on the first play.
- **The third turn** (framework row 8), when it is built, takes "¿Quieren postre o café?" → "No, gracias." as one of its first rungs. No content waits on it.

```js
{ es: "postre", en: "dessert", contextEs: "¿Quieren postre o café?", contextEn: "Would you like dessert or coffee?", note: "You will hear this more than you say it. No, gracias is a full answer. The dessert menu is la carta de postres." }
```

- **Card pair (the 11/23 rule):** postre / the check.
- **Sourcing:** "¿Quieren postre o café?" is in a Spanish-language list of waiter phrases (FluentU, Inglés para camareros); a Spain language school gives the same question as "¿Desean algo de postre o café?", asked after the second course (don Quijote). Sourced as a form; which form Barcelona waiters used is Tom's ear.

## 2. THE PLAIN NO: nothing new to teach.

No, gracias is owned from chapter 1 and is the whole answer. The postre note says so. "Nada más, gracias." is not added: it passes no test "No, gracias" fails.

## 3. DONE, AND THE PLATE: one said line, no card.

Notes 21 and 26 are one moment: the learner says they are finished, and that is also what lets the plate go. The staff's own questions (¿Ha terminado? · ¿Puedo retirarle el plato?) are answered with an owned Sí, gracias; the learner recognises terminado from saying it and hears retirar in the reply.

```js
{ es: "Ya he terminado, gracias.", en: "I'm finished, thank you.", hint: [["I'm finished,", "ya he terminado"], ["thank you.", "gracias"]], near: ["I'm ready to order, thank you.", "I'm still eating, thank you."], replies: [["Vale, le retiro el plato", "Okay, I'll take your plate"], ["¿Le traigo la carta de postres?", "Shall I bring you the dessert menu?"], ["¿Un café?", "A coffee?"]], note: "The waiter asks it first as ¿Ha terminado? For the whole table, say Ya hemos terminado." }
```

- **Owed reply:** "¿Le traigo la carta de postres?" is heard on the first play (Tom's note 11 is this question).
- **Singular, with the plural in the note.** "He terminado" said for one plate is never wrong; "hemos terminado" said while someone is still eating is. No card: one line, and its note carries what a card would.
- **No frame tag of its own is needed** beyond a composer-only tag (`terminado`) so it never counts against another tag's three.
- **Sourcing:** retirar is the trade's verb for clearing ("¿Puedo retirarle el plato?", FluentU's busser phrases). "Ya he terminado, gracias." as the diner's line, and all three replies as worded, are chat's own: unsourced, for Tom's ear. "¿Todo bien?" is deliberately not used here (Tom, 11/5: odd beside the check).

## 4. THE DESSERT CARRIER (ask 5)

By the 11/19 rule: a gluten line and a lactose line, nothing for allergies or vegetarian.

- **Who gets which:** Celiac and Gluten-free, not celiac get the gluten line. Lactose intolerant gets the lactose line. Milk allergy, the other allergies, and vegetarian get neither.
- **Where:** both sit in **Your table lines**, last. The standing rule is unchanged (two or more own Table lines make a row), so the lactose-intolerant learner now has a row of two. No exception written.
- **Tag:** a composer-only tag `postre`, so the line never crowds ¿Tiene? in the row.
- **Sourcing:** the pattern is the shipped one (¿Tiene cerveza sin gluten?). Replies are chat's own, unsourced. Flan, sorbete, and fruta are heard only.
