# Tripfluent — Handoff to chat: Sounds you'll say (2026-10-06, v428, STAGED `sounds-1`)

From Code, via Tom. A new chapter-1 session, built and staged on Tom's rulings; everything in it is Code's draft. This handoff asks for two things: a check that the pronunciations are right and defensible, and the voice pass on the lines.

## 1. What the session is (no action)

Chapter 1, third session, after First words · Part 2. It teaches no words: nine KEY SOUND cards, each a carrier word with the sound's letters in gold, the translation as a whisper, the rule as the subtitle, two more carriers (tap one: it is said, and its meaning shows), the speaker with slow playback, and a two-choice confirm ("Tap the pronunciation": the Spanish way against the English-eye way). Then the wall in two rounds ("Find the sound": one word at a time, the key part among decoys). Then the run: nine words said out loud, the mic when the phone offers one (a match dings; the word turns green). Then an ending of nine tiles. The carriers never enter review. Pill: BEST SAID OUT LOUD; the escape ("I can't speak right now") offers listening-only rounds of "Which one sounds different?" or saves the session (SAVED FOR SPEAKING).

## 2. THE CHECK: are the pronunciations right, and what do we stand on?

Code's basis: standard Castilian (Spain) phonetics, the norm the Real Academia describes and every Castilian course teaches: the *distinción* (c before e/i and z as [θ]), j and g before e/i as [x], h silent, *yeísmo* (ll as [ʝ]), b and v as one sound, qu as [k] with a silent u, the tap [ɾ] against the trill [r], five pure vowels, stress on the second-to-last syllable unless the mark says otherwise. The IPA for each carrier is in the pack beside its say line. The say lines are English-reader respellings of that IPA: capitals mark the stressed part, hyphens divide the parts. They are approximations by nature (English has no Spanish e or o); the app's es-ES voice is what the learner actually hears.

**Chat to confirm or correct, per row:** the IPA, the say line as a fair English rendering, the decoy as the mistake an English reader really makes, the carrier as a fair example.

## 3. THE VOICE PASS (Code's drafts)

- Session line: "Nine sounds that trip an English eye. Hear them, then say them."
- The nine rule lines: 1 "The c here is a soft th in Spain. The lisp is the accent, not a mistake." · 2 "The j is rough, from the throat, like clearing it. The g before e or i does the same." · 3 "The h is silent, always. Write it, never say it." · 4 "The ll is the y in yes." · 5 "The ñ is the ny in canyon." · 6 "The v sounds like a b." · 7 "The qu is a plain k. The u is silent." · 8 "One r is a quick tap of the tongue. Two are a roll." · 9 "Five vowels, short and pure, never slid. Lean on the second-to-last part, or on the part with the mark."
- The card: label KEY SOUND · "Tap to hear it, or hear it slowly" · "Tap the pronunciation".
- The wall: "Find the sound" · "The part in gold: how does it sound?" · "N of 5 found" · "Every sound, found." · "One took a second look."
- The run: "Say it" · "N of 9 said" · "Hear it, or say it to the mic" / "Say it, then tap to compare" · Again / Said it · "Listening…" · "Hearing you…" · THAT'S IT "You said jamón." · "We heard "X". Try it once more, or move on." · "The microphone did not catch that."
- The escape: "I can't speak right now" · "This one is built for your voice." · "Listen for now, or come back when you can speak. It will be waiting on Home." · "Keep going, just listening" / "Come back when I can speak" · "Just listen for now".
- Which one sounds different? (the listening path): "Three of these share a sound." · "Read them. Tap the one that does not belong." · the seven reveal lines: casa "In casa the c comes before an a: a hard k, not the soft th." · gato "In gato the g comes before an a: a plain g, not the rough sound." · jamón (for h) "The h in the other three is silent. The j in jamón is the one you hear." · sal "One l is a plain l. Only the double ll turns into the y sound." · nada "A plain n. Only the ñ with its mark carries the ny." · cuándo "With cu the u is said: kw. With qu it is silent: k." · pero "One r: a single tap. Pero means but; perro means dog."
- The ending: "Nine sounds, said out loud." · "You will hear every one of them in the next sessions. Now you know what you are hearing."
- Pills: BEST SAID OUT LOUD · SAVED FOR SPEAKING.

## 4. Rulings to note (Tom, 10/6)

The session is introduction first, a little testing at the end; it never enters review; a hint never gives the thing under test (so no speaker before the answer on Which one sounds different?); an introduction is never a test (the card's confirm is two choices, ungraded).
