# Tripfluent, brief for chat: the dietary lines, allergy by allergy (Code, 2026-11-19)

*Tom read every dietary line at once (tripfluent-dietary-lines-review.md, generated from the engine) and found the design's real flaw: the app treats every dietary answer the same way. One set of "sin ___" sentences, written for gluten, takes each learner's word in the slot. His words: "Gluten needs to be wide sweeping, peanuts not so much." This brief is Code's reasoning, allergy by allergy, for chat to verify and write. It is safety content, so everything below marked as reasoning needs a source before it becomes copy. It replaces asks 8 and 9 of the 11/18 follow-ups note (tripfluent-chat-handoff-1118b-dietary-followups.md); asks 1 to 7 there stand.*

## What Tom said (11/19)

1. "Trazas is the label's word for traces": he did not realize "label" meant food packaging in Spain. Say it is what is written on food labels in Spain, or say the legal requirement if that is the case.
2. Asked "are there tapas without dairy?", the app must not answer "only with meat." An appropriate response for each allergy.
3. Do we need "do you have tapas without peanuts?" at all? Most tapas have none; someone just needs to ask whether a specific thing has peanuts. Think through every place variation by allergy and decide whether each needs specific asks or wide sweeping ones.
4. Confirm the traces question. Is "can it have traces of fish?" a real question? Think it through for every allergy.

## What the app does today

Five carrier sentences take the learner's word in the slot, with the replies written for the base (gluten, or meat for the tapas line):

| Session | Carrier | Offered to |
|---|---|---|
| Counter | ¿Tiene algo sin ___? | every answer |
| Counter | ¿Hay tapas sin ___? | the seven allergies (base: vegetarian) |
| Table | ¿Hay algo sin ___ en la carta? | every answer |
| Table | ¿Tiene pan sin ___? | dairy, eggs, soy, nuts (the rest get ¿Tiene tapas sin ___?) |
| Shop | ¿Tiene chocolate sin ___? | dairy, nuts, peanuts, eggs, soy |

So a peanut learner is taught to ask a bar for tapas without peanuts and a shop for chocolate without peanuts, and hears "Solo con carne" back. Each allergy learner also has, from batch 2b: ¿Lleva ___?, Tengo alergia a ___, No puedo comer ___, ¿Puede llevar trazas de ___?

## Code's reasoning, by answer (to verify)

| Answer | How common in Spanish bar and restaurant food | Where it hides | Sweeping or specific |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gluten-free, celiac | Everywhere: bread with everything, batter, croquetas, beer | flour in sauces, shared fryers | **Sweeping**, plus specific |
| Vegetarian | Everywhere: ham, tuna, meat stock | jamón in vegetable dishes, tuna in salads, caldo | **Sweeping** (by vegetariano), plus ham and tuna by name |
| Shellfish | Common, more so on the coast | fumet or caldo in rice dishes, croquetas, a shared plancha | Mostly specific; one sweeping line |
| Fish | Common | anchovies in olives and salads, tuna in ensaladilla and empanada, fish stock | Mostly specific; one sweeping line |
| Dairy | Moderate (the cooking is olive-oil based) | béchamel in croquetas, butter, cream sauces, desserts | Mostly specific; one sweeping line |
| Eggs | Moderate | mayonnaise and alioli, batter, flan, crema catalana | Specific |
| Nuts | Rare in tapas; common in Catalan sauces and in desserts | romesco, picada, ajoblanco, turrón, tarta de Santiago | Specific, and the sauce by name |
| Peanuts | Rare in the cooking itself | snack bowls on the bar, ice cream, Asian kitchens | Specific only |
| Soy | Rare in traditional food | soy sauce, processed food, some industrial bread | Specific only |

## Proposals

**A. Three tiers instead of one slot.**
- *Sweeping* (gluten-free, celiac, vegetarian): keep the place carriers, in their own words (vegetarian by vegetariano, ask 7 of the earlier note).
- *One sweeping line* (shellfish, fish, dairy): keep ¿Hay algo sin ___ en la carta? at the Table only; drop the Counter, bread, and Shop carriers.
- *Specific only* (eggs, nuts, peanuts, soy): no "sin ___" carriers at all.

**B. One sweeping ask that is real for every allergy:** ¿Tiene la carta de alérgenos? (Do you have the allergen menu?). Restaurants in Spain must be able to give the allergens in each dish, and chat's own 2b reply set already has a waiter offering "la carta de alérgenos." For the specific-only tier it replaces every invented "tapas without ___" line with a question that gets a document. Chat to verify the duty and the everyday name of the thing.

**C. Replies written per line, per tier.** With the carriers pruned, each remaining sentence keeps replies that are true for the answers it is offered to. No reply names a dish that could carry the learner's allergen ("Sí, la ensalada" is wrong for nuts and for fish).

**D. Where it hides, as a Good to know on each allergy's ¿Lleva ___? card.** The most useful safety fact per allergy is not a sweep but a hiding place. Code's prompts, each to be verified:
- Nuts: in Catalonia, romesco and picada are made with nuts. Ask about the sauce.
- Shellfish and fish: rice dishes are often cooked in fish or shellfish stock.
- Eggs: mayonnaise and alioli; ensaladilla is bound with mayonnaise.
- Dairy: croquetas are béchamel. (The sin lactosa note stays.)
- Peanuts: the snack bowl on the bar.
- Fish: anchovies inside stuffed olives.

**E. The kitchen question, by cluster, not one "trazas" line for all.**
- Nuts and peanuts: ¿Puede llevar trazas de ___? stands; traces is the natural word and it is on every package.
- Fish and shellfish: the vector is a shared fryer or plancha, so the question is whether it is cooked in the same oil (the fryer line Tom asked for under celiac, ask 2 of the earlier note, may be the same sentence for all three).
- Gluten: ¿Hay contaminación cruzada? and the fryer.
- Dairy, eggs, soy: none of their own unless chat finds one that is really asked.

**F. The trazas note, reworded (Tom's point 1).** "The label" was ambiguous. It is the precautionary line on food packaging, and it is voluntary: the law covers the listed allergens as ingredients, not unintended traces (chat's own 2b finding). Code's draft: "Trazas is the word printed on Spanish food packaging: puede contener trazas de ___, may contain traces of ___. If you hear No lo puedo garantizar, they cannot promise the dish is safe." Worth a read-only sign too: the package line itself, in Signs you'll read or the Shop, for nut and peanut learners.

**G. Each answer's own everyday line, where one exists.**
- Lactose-free: ¿Tiene leche sin lactosa? at the Counter (the café question), in place of the generic carriers.
- Gluten: ¿Tiene cerveza sin gluten? beside ¿Tiene pan sin gluten? (caña is a Counter word).
