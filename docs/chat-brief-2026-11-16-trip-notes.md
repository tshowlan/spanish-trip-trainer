*Tom is back from the trip the app was built for, with thirty notes. They are numbered and checked against the pack in tripfluent-trip-notes.md (attached). This brief groups them into nine batches and asks for three rulings on premise plus the writing for batch 1 only. ## What the notes say together

1. **The table is where the trip happened.** Fourteen of thirty notes are at a table. The Table session teaches arriving and ordering for one. It has nothing for a group, nothing for asking again, nothing for the meal's end.
2. **The Street was written for a traveler without a phone.** Five notes say nobody asks a stranger how far the station is. The phone answers where. The traveler needs "here" and a way to confirm.
3. **Paying skips a step:** where you pay, and the question that comes with the card machine.
4. **Barcelona's signs speak Catalan too.** The app's Spain is Castilian only.

## Three rulings on premise (these shape the batches)

**P1. The Street's premise: a phone in the hand.** Tom: "We should assume our users are using smartphone maps." Notes 23, 29, 30 cut three sentences (¿Está lejos la estación?, ¿Está cerca el hotel?, ¿Hay taxis en la estación?). Notes 1 and 28 add the pointing lines ("Can I go here?", "To here, please.", "To this address."). This reaches the Street's own frame, ¿Está ___?, which keeps its use with abierto, cerrado, and libre but loses far and near as questions about places; and the taxi card chat wrote on 11/5b (Al hotel / A la estación), which Tom now reads as the wrong line: with Uber and a map the job is confirming the destination, not naming it. Rule on the premise, then what the Street's frame is for.

**P2. Ordering for the table.** Tom ordered tapas for a group all week and the app only has Para mí, ___. Is "for us" a new place frame at the Table (Code's prompts: Para compartir, ___; Nos pone ___; Para nosotros, ___), a second frame beside Para mí, or sentences under an existing ask? The one-frame-per-place law is at stake, as it was with the taxi.

**P3. How much Catalan.** Tom heard "bon dia" often and read Serveis and Lavabos on doors where the app taught Servicios and Aseos. Options: (a) Good to know notes only; (b) sign variants on the plates that have one (Sortida, Entrada, Obert, Tancat, Serveis, Lavabos) for learners whose destination is Barcelona or Catalonia; (c) also a few heard-only greetings in exercises (bon dia, adéu, gràcies, si us plau). The pack is "Spain"; this would be the first content keyed to a city. Tom's floor: at least a Good to know, and things people say in some exercises.
